Selected Work · Armory

Winning a ₹100Cr order with no paid marketing

Raised Armory’s marketing from 0 to present day — under a hard constraint: you can’t show the product.

Decisions that mattered

Deeptech is tough, tougher still when you can’t talk about the product you are building and also want to sell. Internet however doesn’t care. If the brand doesn’t have a presence, it is invisible. At Armory, we were building SURGE, a Counter drone system from scratch — something not done in India at the pace we did. The constraint? ‘You can’t show the product’, ‘You can’t share pictures of engineers working’ and the ask? ‘Get us followers relevant to our domain’. You can say on the internet, ‘hey we are building the best thing to come out of the country’ but that does not draw engagement.

I took 3 decisions to nail this task —

  1. Find influencers in Deep-tech — a niche industry and the beauty of niche industry is: passionate followers.
  2. Get the founder to speak on Twitter spaces hosted by those Deep-tech bigwigs and share his previous experience from another successful startup to gain faith and momentum.
  3. Go to offline expos, find customer using their military badge — if it is a decision maker, chase them to give you 2 mins and understand what they want and pitch what you building — request for a demonstration.

I won on all fronts.

We instantly scaled to 1000 followers within 4 months with 0 product pictures — purely through twitter spaces and creating a secretive aura of what we are about to do. 1000 followers seem small but only when you put preference to the vanity metric without knowing who those 1000 followers actually were. We got people working in defence, journalists and people who loved the idea of building in India following and talking about us.

We successfully converted 7 demonstration requests at defence expos and asked them for a time when they would want to see our product in action.

We ensured we built relationships at each of those demonstrations — took cards, contacts, I interviewed them about the problems they face with existing tech — is it bulky? how is the UI of competitors? Arrived first at each demo, left last.

What I’d do differently

You can fix everything in hindsight but some misses still bother me. We didn’t have a great system to showcase our product. When I was told we couldn’t shoot product video — I completely misunderstood that as a hard stop when in fact it was only for social media. We would have had a harder impact during these meetups had I carried a video proof.

Future meet ups — I created a prototype of product UI, video on my iPad and we were the best prepped team amongst all.

Lesson: when someone says 'you can't,' ask 'where can't I' before accepting it.

Results

  • 1,000 domain-relevant followers in 4 months with zero product pictures — defence practitioners, journalists, and India-builders.
  • 7 demonstration requests converted at defence expos.
  • Field relationships built demo by demo — cards, contacts, and competitor UX intel gathered in person.
  • Contributed to Armory landing a ₹100Cr+ Ministry of Defence order with no paid marketing.
