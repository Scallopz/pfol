Defence Expo Outreach Playbook

A structured system to identify and engage high-value defence stakeholders at large-scale expos, contributing to a pipeline that led to ₹100Cr ($10M+) in contracts.

Context

At defence expos, thousands of attendees, vendors, and officials move through a chaotic environment. Conversations are random, attention is limited, and missing the right person means missing real opportunity. This playbook highlights how you can identify key stakeholders and close a sales lead even without a stall at the expo.

Problem

Most outreach at expos is reactive — people talk to whoever is available or whoever has stopped by their stall. But what if you don't have a stall? Better still, you know stall isn't what you need at the stage your company is in.

This outreach leads to generic outcomes.

Your conversations are low quality and are often with people who aren't your customers.

You miss engagement with senior decision makers — people who have the final say in whether your product will see a sale.

This is throwing darts in the air.

I am lazy and I seek optimisation at everything I do. We need a way to systematically identify and engage the right people in real time.

System

Here is what I did:

Stakeholder Mapping

Mapped defence hierarchy and identified which ranks and roles held actual decision-making power.

Visual Recognition System

Built a quick-reference system (using Figma) to memorize ranks, insignia, and visual cues. This allows instant identification on the floor.

Priority-Based Engagement

Shifted from random outreach to targeted interaction, focusing only on high-value individuals. You talk to people who are actively only engaging with similar companies or products.

Communication Compression

Refined product messaging into short, high-context explanations that could be delivered in under a minute + a visual handbook that showcases your product.

Real-Time Adaptation

Adjusted approach dynamically based on crowd flow, event type, and stakeholder behavior.

Ensure a follow up

Your conversation should end with a phone number or a commitment to a meeting.

Outcome

This outreach helped us ace meetings and discussions with several key stakeholders. What started as conversations to showcase our products, ended up leading to ₹100Cr ($10M+) in defence contracts.

  • Significantly improved quality of stakeholder engagement
  • Reduced wasted interactions in high-noise environments

In high-stakes environments, recognition and prioritization matter more than volume of outreach.
